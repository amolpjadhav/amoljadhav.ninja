# Inside Amazon's Q2 2026 Earnings: A Great Quarter Wearing a $53 Billion Costume

A summary of Amazon's Q2 2026 earnings report — the quarter ended June 30, 2026, reported July 30, 2026.

**Source:** Amazon's [Q2 2026 press release](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Second-Quarter-Results/), earnings call transcript, and investor presentation deck.

### Highlights

- Net sales: $200.6B, up 20% year-over-year, beating the ~$196.5B analyst consensus.
- AWS revenue grew 37% to $42.2B — its fastest growth in 18 quarters — with AWS operating margin expanding 650 basis points.
- AWS's AI and chips businesses each now exceed a $25B annual revenue run rate, both growing at triple-digit percentages.
- Advertising revenue grew 26% year-over-year.

### Lowlights

- Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow swung negative for the first time in this window: a $7.6B outflow, down from an $18.2B inflow a year ago.
- Full-year 2026 cash capex guidance was raised to about $220 billion (from ~$200 billion), due to higher memory-chip prices.
- Nearly all of the quarter's massive net-income growth came from a single $53.4 billion non-operating investment gain, not the operating business.
- Q3 guidance implies real deceleration (9–12% growth vs. 20% this quarter), though management attributes most of that to Prime Day timing and currency.

Look at that "Other income (expense), net" row: $53.42 billion, up from $1.12 billion a year ago. Amazon's own disclosure is direct about it — this quarter's net income "includes non-operating pre-tax other income of $53.4 billion, primarily from our investments in Anthropic." Strip that one line out, and income before taxes would have been about $27.4 billion — almost exactly equal to operating income, which is what you'd expect. The other $53.4 billion is a mark-to-market gain on a private investment stake, not sales, not profit from running the business.

**A rougher, honest comparison:** if you tax that $27.4 billion "real" pretax income at this quarter's own 22.5% effective rate, normalized net income comes out to roughly $21.3 billion, or about **$1.95 per diluted share** — this is our own estimate, not a figure Amazon reports. That's a modest beat over the ~$1.82 analysts were modeling, not the 3x "beat" the headline $5.75 implies.

### The tension

The headline says profit nearly quadrupled. It didn't — not from the business, anyway. The actual operating business had a genuinely strong quarter (margin expanded 2.3 points, AWS margin expanded 6.5 points), but free cash flow — the number hardest to dress up — just went negative for the first time in years, and it's been getting worse for five straight quarters running.

The free cash flow trend is the one that matters most this quarter, so here it is directly, trailing twelve months: **$18.2B → $14.8B → $11.2B → $1.2B → ($7.6B)**. Five straight quarters of decline, crossing from comfortably positive to outright negative, driven — in Amazon's own words — "primarily by a year-over-year increase of $66.1 billion in purchases of property and equipment... this increase primarily reflects investments in artificial intelligence."

### Where the growth is coming from

All three segments grew, and the fastest one keeps getting faster:

- **AWS:** $42.2B, up 37% — its fastest growth in 18 quarters, marking a fifth consecutive quarter of acceleration. AWS operating income grew 64% to $16.6B, with margin expanding 650 basis points — the spending is visibly converting into profit, not just cost.
- **North America:** $116.2B, up 16%, with operating income up 21% to $9.1B.
- **International:** $42.2B, up 15% (18% on an FX-adjusted basis), with operating income up 15% to $1.7B.
- **Advertising** (spanning the retail segments): revenue grew 26% year-over-year.

CEO Andy Jassy's framing, direct from the release: "AWS is booming, growing 36.7% year-over-year in Q2 — our fastest growth in 18 quarters — and our AI and Chips businesses each eclipsed run rates of more than $25 billion." On why AWS keeps accelerating despite the scale it's already at, he pointed to unmet demand rather than market saturation: "we now believe we will spend approximately $220 billion in cash CapEx in 2026... [and] even at that amount, we will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026. Demand we already have for 2028 is striking."

### Where the money's going

Total operating expenses grew 17%, slower than revenue's 20% — which is exactly why operating margin expanded instead of compressing:

| Expense line | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost of sales** | $95.78B | $80.81B | +19% |
| Technology and infrastructure | $33.16B | $27.17B | +22% |
| Fulfillment | $29.63B | $25.98B | +14% |
| Sales and marketing | $11.70B | $11.42B | +2% |
| General and administrative | $2.79B | $2.97B | −6% |
| **Total operating expenses** | **$173.15B** | **$148.53B** | **+17%** |

Cost of sales is the largest dollar increase (+$15.0B), simply tracking Amazon's overall size. But **Technology and infrastructure — the line where AWS and AI capacity costs concentrate — grew faster than revenue itself (22% vs. 20%)**, the same pattern showing up at every AI-heavy company right now. The one line moving the other direction is G&A, down 6% — actual cost discipline, not just slower growth.

### The cash story

This is where the quarter stops looking uniformly great:

- **Capital expenditures** were $53.1 billion in cash for the quarter alone, "primarily relates to AWS and generative AI," per CFO Brian Olsavsky.
- **Full-year 2026 capex guidance** was raised to approximately $220 billion, up from the prior ~$200 billion estimate — Jassy attributed the increase specifically to "the higher cost of memory."
- **Free cash flow (trailing twelve months)** swung to a $7.6 billion outflow, from an $18.2 billion inflow a year earlier — even as operating cash flow itself grew 33% to $161.4 billion. The capital bill grew faster than the cash coming in, by a wide margin.
- **Cash and equivalents** stood at $78.2 billion as of June 30, 2026.

Management isn't hiding from this. Jassy addressed it directly: "In the short term... we'll spend a lot of CapEx and encounter free cash flow headwinds until data centers come online. [But a] few years out... revenue growth outpaces incremental CapEx growth, [and] the resulting free cash flow is very compelling." That's a real bet on a multi-year payoff, not a denial that the near-term cash picture has gotten worse.

### What guidance says

- **Q3 2026 net sales:** $197.0–202.0 billion, 9–12% growth — a real deceleration from this quarter's 20%. Amazon says excluding the Prime Day timing shift between years, growth would run nearly 400 basis points higher, plus an ~80 basis point currency headwind.
- **Q3 2026 operating income:** $22.5–26.5 billion, versus $17.4 billion a year ago (29–52% growth) — a $24.5B midpoint that lands close to the ~$24.9B analysts were already modeling.
- No new quarterly capex figure was given beyond the raised full-year ~$220 billion guide.

The growth deceleration in the headline number is mostly a timing and currency artifact, not a demand problem — but it's still true that Q3's guided growth rate is meaningfully lower than what just printed.
