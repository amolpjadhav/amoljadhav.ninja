# How Much Money Is There, Really? The World's Cash vs. the World's Wealth

Imagine someone asked you: how much money is there in the world? You'd probably guess there's one answer. There isn't — there are two totally different answers, and mixing them up is where most of the confusion about money starts.

The first answer is about real money. Think of every dollar bill, every coin, and every dollar sitting in someone's bank account. Add all of that up, and you get money someone could actually spend today.

The second answer is completely different. It's not cash at all. It's a guess at how much *stuff* is worth — houses, gold, and tiny pieces of companies called stocks. Nobody's holding any of that as cash in a wallet. It's just what people think it would sell for.

Most people picture these as one big pile. They're not two views of the same pile — they're two different piles, and the gap between them is enormous.

*A quick note before we start: every number below comes from a real source — a central bank, a research firm, or an industry group — never a guess. We'll say where each one comes from as we go. Every currency is converted into dollars using today's exchange rate, so everything lines up side by side.*

---

### What counts as "real money"?

Economists have a nickname for the cash-and-bank-account pile: **M2**. It's just a label for one specific number, and it only counts a few things:

- Paper cash and coins
- Money sitting in checking accounts
- Money sitting in savings accounts
- Money in something called a money-market fund (basically a savings account for grown-ups)

M2 does **not** count your house, your stocks, or your retirement account. Just the money you could walk up to a bank and actually take out, today.

That's the number worth starting with, because it's the closest thing to "how much money could actually be spent right now."

Add every country together, and the whole world has about **$123 trillion** in real, spendable money. That's every dollar, yuan, euro, yen, and pound sitting in cash or a bank account, anywhere on Earth, right now.

Remember that number: **$123 trillion**. Now let's look at something very different.

### Now here's the confusing part

Stocks, bonds, houses, and gold are not money. Nobody carries a stock around like a dollar bill. They're called **assets** — things people own that someone else would pay money for.

Nobody knows the *exact* price of an asset until someone actually buys it. But experts can make a very good estimate, based on what similar things recently sold for. Stocks, bonds, real estate (a fancy word for land and buildings), and gold together add up to roughly **$722 trillion**. That's about **six times** the entire world's real money.

### Why is "wealth" so much bigger than "money"?

This is the part that trips almost everyone up, so let's slow down.

That $722 trillion isn't sitting anywhere, waiting to be spent. It's not a giant vault of cash. It's an estimate — a very educated guess about what things would sell for, added up.

Here's a small version of the same idea. Say your neighbor's house sold last year for $400,000. Now everyone calls the house "worth $400,000." But there isn't an extra $400,000 in cash sitting around just because people say that. If your neighbor wants to sell it again tomorrow, they still need to find one real buyer with $400,000 in actual money, willing to hand it over.

Now do that for every house, every stock, and every gold bar on the planet. That's the gap you saw above: $722 trillion in *estimates*, sitting on top of only $123 trillion in money that actually exists to pay for any of it.

This is also why "everyone sells at once" never works the way it sounds. Picture every homeowner and every shareholder on Earth trying to sell on the same day. There isn't enough real money in the world to pay everyone the price their thing was "worth" the day before. Prices would crash fast — not because the houses or stocks got worse, but because the cash to pay full price for all of it was never actually there.

So: how much stuff is worth, and how much real money exists, are two different questions. One is a price tag. The other is what's actually in people's pockets to pay it.

### Why this matters for you

Here's the big takeaway. "The stock market is worth $150 trillion" sounds like it means there's $150 trillion in cash sitting somewhere. It doesn't. Those are two very different sentences, and only the first one is true.

The same goes for your own life. A savings account, a house, an investment your family owns — add it all up and you get something called **net worth**. But net worth is a price tag, not a pile of cash. It's real, and it's exactly as real as the buyer who'd actually pay for it — but it was never money sitting in a vault, and it was never going to be.
